Landed costs and ledger postings

Finance users enter landed costs (freight, duty) on a goods receipt. They are allocated across lines by value, and the shares always add up exactly.
Receipts, dispatches, customer and supplier returns, and stock adjustments now post to the ledger. Receipts debit Inventory and credit Goods received not invoiced. Dispatches debit Cost of sales and credit Inventory.
This switches on only once four accounts are mapped: inventory, cost of sales, goods received not invoiced, and inventory adjustment. Until then stock stays quantity-only, as before. A fifth account, purchase price variance, is mapped separately.
Stock received before valuation was switched on can't be issued until it gets an opening valuation. The offset goes to the inventory adjustment account and should be reviewed by an accountant.
Each stock pool holds a single currency. Receiving in another currency while stock is on hand is refused.
Purchasing controls

Admins set an approval threshold. Orders at or above it, and any order in a non-default currency, wait for a finance user other than the creator before goods can be received. Rejection needs a comment, and decisions are kept in an append-only history.
A vendor bill can be linked to a purchase order. It's matched against the value of goods received, using order prices, plus landed costs. A larger bill needs an override reason and is stored as an "exception".
Matched bills clear the Goods received not invoiced accrual, and any excess is expensed as purchase price variance.
The sub-ledger reconciliation now also compares stock value and the accrual with their ledger accounts.
UI. The Inventory page has a Valuation tab for finance users: stock value, opening valuation, and the approval threshold for admins. Purchase orders get Approve and Reject buttons. The goods receipt form has a landed-cost field. The Finance bill form has a purchase-order selector, and the account mappings include the five new accounts.

Tax and sign-offs

Under Settings → Jurisdiction, an administrator records, per country and scope (tax or numbering), the adviser's name, firm, the opinion they relied on, the date, and for tax the rounding method they confirmed. Sign-offs can be revoked with a reason.
Until a sign-off exists for the organisation's country, invoices behave exactly as before: no tax, and ID-based numbers.
The sign-off only stores the claim; the application cannot check it.
Once tax is on, tax is computed on the server in integer minor units. Two rounding methods are available: once per rate, or per line.
Each invoice stores its tax breakdown. Output tax posts to a new tax-payable account.
The reference rates (LV 21%, LT 21%, EE 24%, DE 19%, GB 20%) are unverified starting values for your adviser to confirm.
Numbering and corrections

With a numbering sign-off, invoices and credit notes get gapless numbers from a locked counter, for example INV-LV-2026-000001. A failed issue consumes no number.
I ran eight parallel issues on PostgreSQL, including the first issue of a year. The numbers came out consecutive with no gaps or duplicates.
Issued invoices are never edited. Credit notes are the correction path.
A credit note can't exceed what is still owed, and can't exceed the net invoiced per tax code. Refunding money already received isn't supported.
Invoice and credit-note PDFs now show frozen seller and customer details, the tax breakdown and payment instructions. Issuing needs the organisation's legal name and address once either sign-off is on.
Bank workflow

I chose provider-agnostic statement import, camt.053 XML and CSV, because it needs no bank credentials and works across all five countries.
Lines are matched by exact amount and currency to recorded receipts and vendor payments. A matching reference in the bank text lets auto-match pair them.
Lines with no book record, such as bank fees, are ignored with a recorded reason.
A period report lists unmatched items on both sides.
Re-importing a file, or importing overlapping statements, doesn't duplicate transactions.
A statement whose balances don't add up is rejected, as is XML with entity declarations.
Nothing connects to a bank or posts to the ledger from here.
Foreign exchange. Left out on purpose. Everything stays single-currency. The decision and what would trigger it are in docs/ACCOUNTING_TREATMENT.md.

I've added a new section to README.md, P2 — Document management (DMS) module, placed just before "Later". It's only roadmap text: no code was written and nothing is committed.

The org structure has to come first. The app has no units, sections, positions or reporting lines today, and roles are only ROLE_USER/FINANCE/INVENTORY/ADMIN. Approval by subordination and sending documents to units need that structure, so it's Phase 1.

The five phases:

Org structure: a tree of units and sections, positions, and employees assigned to positions. It includes a service that works out each user's chain of superiors and subordinates, handles vacant head posts, and supports deputies for absences.
Document registry: document types, a lifecycle (draft through archived), registration numbers per type and year, read-only past versions with check-out locking, attachments and links between documents, preview and printable approval sheets, access control including confidential documents, and full-text search.
Approval by subordination: sequential and parallel steps. The server only accepts approvers from the sender's own chain of superiors, and nobody can approve their own document. Approvers can approve, reject with a reason, return for revision or forward for review. Every action is kept in a history that can't be edited. Overdue approvals send reminders and escalate to the approver's superior. Includes an "Awaiting my approval" inbox and a route timeline.
Acknowledgement: managers send documents to units or sections (optionally including sub-units), positions or individual people, but only within the part of the organisation they manage. Admins can reach everyone. The recipient list is saved when the request is sent. Each person moves from pending to viewed to acknowledged, or is marked overdue or excused. Managers get a completion dashboard by unit with drill-down to employees, CSV export, reminders, and a fresh acknowledgement round when a document gets a new version.
Lifecycle and hardening: effective, review and expiry dates, retention periods, legal hold, a full audit trail, reports, virus scanning and backups, and tests.
Open decisions at the top of the section that you should settle first:

Who counts as "management staff": I proposed unit and section heads acting within their own part of the organisation, plus a new DMS admin role.
How approval routes are chosen: the sender picks from their own superiors, routes are generated from per-type templates, or both.
Whether a simple electronic confirmation is enough: the alternative is qualified e-signatures (eIDAS).
Retention periods.
Things like incoming/outgoing mail registration, OCR and co-authoring are listed as out of scope. Email notifications are also out of scope because the mail library is still blocked by Composer's security policy, as already noted in P1.

Phase 3, the approval workflow, is in place on top of your dms phase 2 commit; this round isn't committed. The new screens haven't been checked in a browser, since none was available in this session.

How approval works now:

Sending: the author (or a registry clerk) builds a route of stages. Stages run one after another; everyone in a stage is asked at once, and the stage completes when everyone agrees or on the first approval, as chosen. Each participant can have a due date. A document type can also come with a default route, built from levels up the author's reporting line.
Subordination rules: approve and sign steps can only go to the author's superiors, and the server refuses anyone else. Review steps can go to any colleague who holds a position, for things like legal or finance checks. Nobody can approve their own document.
Approver actions: approve (with an optional comment), reject (reason required), or return for revision. An approver can also ask one of their own subordinates for an opinion; that opinion is advisory and the decision stays with the approver.
Author actions: withdraw, add a reviewer to the stage that's running, or send again after a rejection. Per type, a resubmission either runs every stage again or keeps the stages already approved.
Deputies: an active deputy sees the absent person's steps in their inbox and decides on their behalf. Both names are recorded. This completes the Phase 1 delegation item.
Record-keeping: every action goes into a history that can't be edited, with who acted, on whose behalf, the comment and the exact version's fingerprint. Each step also keeps the person's position and unit as they were at the time.
Registration: after the final approval, a document is either registered automatically or left for the clerk, depending on the type. The seeded Order type routes to the direct superior for approval, then one level up for signature. Policy, Instruction, Regulation and Contract route to the direct superior.
Deadlines: the existing reminder scheduler now sends due-soon and overdue notices for approval steps. A step that stays overdue escalates to the approver's own superior. Notifications link straight to the document.
Comments and comparison: comments are attached to a version and can quote a passage. They are resolved rather than deleted. Two versions can be compared sentence by sentence, but only Word, Excel, OpenDocument and text files: PDFs and images have no extracted text yet.
Screens:

Documents section: a new "Awaiting me" tab with a counter.

Document page: an Approval panel with:

controls to decide, forward, withdraw and add reviewers;

It also gets a Comments panel with version comparison.

Document types: clerks can edit the default route, automatic registration and the resubmission mode.
